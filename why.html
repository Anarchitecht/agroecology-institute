We do not need a website for any of that. What happens if you don't have a website? Banks, insurers, grant programs, government registries, payment processors, and credentialing systems all balk at dealing with an organization that doesn't have one, making refusal costly enough to be a compulsory display of “legitimacy”. No law requires a website, but a social norm that nobody privately supports and ineffective security kayfabe and compliance theater can bind more tightly than legislation. The empirical expectations (what you see others doing) and normative expectations (what you believe others think you should do) can both be wrong simultaneously: everyone complies, everyone assumes everyone else approves, even if nobody actually does.

If you are a bureaucrat discerning whether we are real and not scoundrels, we will plant a tree in your honor for free and text you a photo of it being planted with someone holding a piece of paper with your name written on it. The tree will produce oxygen you need to breathe, remove CO2 from the atmosphere, and create food and habitat for animals. You can even arrange a visit.

The web has existed a mere 0.011% of Homo sapiens' 315,000 years. When a website as a representation becomes more socially powerful than the real thing it represents, the website is no longer an appropriate technology aiding a healthy functioning society; it is a dystopian simulacrum replacing it. Is it suspicious and unprofessional to not have a website when it doesn't directly serve a charitable purpose and runs counter to that purpose?
